# Research questions: the Florida harvester ant (*Pogonomyrmex badius*)

The numbers the simulation needs. For each answer, write a range if there is one, the unit, and the source.
If a number cannot be found for *P. badius*, use the red harvester ant (*P. barbatus*) and mark it **borrowed**.
"Could not find it" is a valid answer; we will pick a value and mark it **our guess**.

Sections A to C are essential. D and E shape what is seen on screen. F to H add depth.

## A. Life cycle and lifespan

1. Worker lifespan (weeks or months).
2. Do foragers die sooner than nest workers? How long does a worker live once it starts foraging?
3. Queen lifespan (years).
4. Days as an egg, as a larva, as a pupa.
5. Share of eggs that reach adulthood.
6. Male lifespan, and when males appear.

## B. Queen and colony growth

7. How a colony starts: does the queen raise the first workers alone? How many, and how long does it take?
8. Eggs laid per day, in a young colony and in a mature one.
9. Colony size after one year, and at maturity (number of workers).
10. Years to reach maturity.
11. Does egg laying stop for part of the year? Which months?
12. What happens when the queen dies? Is she ever replaced?
13. When are new queens and males produced, and when is the mating flight (month, time of day, weather)?

## C. Food

14. Which seeds do they collect? What else do they eat (dead insects, anything sweet)?
15. Who eats what: larvae compared with adults.
16. Food per ant per day, or per colony per day (milligrams, or seeds).
17. Seeds collected per day, or per forager per trip.
18. How many seeds a colony keeps in store, and how long that store lasts.
19. Weight a forager can carry compared with its own body weight. Body weight and length of a worker.
20. How long a worker survives without food.
21. Who opens or processes seeds? (See question 25.)
22. How is food passed between ants and to the larvae?

## D. Roles, sizes and ages

23. The jobs in the colony (nursing, seed work, nest building, foraging, guarding, waste) and the rough share of workers in each.
24. Share of workers that forage. Share that are idle at any moment.
25. Major workers (the large-headed ones): what share of the colony are they, how big are they, and what do they actually do?
26. Does a worker's job change with age? At what age does it move up toward the surface and start foraging?
27. Where in the nest do young workers, older workers, brood and the queen stay?

## E. Foraging

28. Distance foragers travel from the nest (metres).
29. Walking speed (centimetres per second).
30. Length of one foraging trip (minutes), and trips per forager per day.
31. Do they forage alone or along shared trails? How are others recruited to a good patch?
32. How long does a trail scent last?
33. What decides whether a forager leaves the nest? (Gordon's *P. barbatus* work on returning foragers triggering outgoing ones is the likely source; mark it borrowed.)
34. Hours of activity in a day, and the soil-surface temperatures at which they stop.

## F. The nest (the plaster and metal casts)

35. Total depth of a mature nest (metres), and of a young colony's nest.
36. Number of chambers in a mature nest, and total chamber floor area.
37. Chamber shape and size (width, height in centimetres), near the surface and deep down.
38. How chamber size and spacing change with depth.
39. The shafts joining chambers: how many, how wide, vertical or spiralling?
40. Number of entrances.
41. What is kept at each depth: seeds, brood, workers, the queen.
42. How nest size grows with colony size (for example, chamber area per 1,000 workers).
43. How fast they dig (volume or weight of sand per day), and how long it takes to dig a whole nest.
44. Nest moves: how often does a colony move, how far, in which season, and how long does the move take?
45. The nest disc on the surface: its size, and the charcoal and other bits they cover it with.
46. Where do the dead and the rubbish go?

## G. Environment (Florida sandhill)

47. Soil type, and why it matters for digging.
48. Temperature range for activity, and the temperature brood is kept at.
49. What they do in winter: do they stop foraging, and for which months?
50. Effect of rain and of drought.
51. How activity and brood change across the year (a month-by-month calendar if one exists).

## H. Death and danger

52. Main predators and enemies, including other ant colonies and neighbours of the same species.
53. Death rate of foragers per day.
54. How the colony reacts to an attack or a disturbed nest.
55. Usual causes of colony death, and the share of new colonies that survive their first year.
56. Territory: distance between neighbouring nests, and whether neighbours fight.

## For the simulation's scale (work out from the answers)

- Centimetres per pixel: from nest depth (35) and worker length (19).
- Real time per simulated minute: from worker lifespan (1) and trip length (30).
- Food units: from food per ant per day (16) and seeds per trip (17).
